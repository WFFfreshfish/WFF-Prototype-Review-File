Resource Rankings

The Lake Rankings provide anglers with a simple way to compare Alabama’s public fisheries based on the qualities that matter most on the water. Rather than relying on a single measurement, each lake is evaluated using information collected during standardized electrofishing fisheries surveys. These measurements are converted into standardized scores so they can be compared fairly across lakes, then combined into rankings based on different angler preferences. Whether your goal is to catch lots of fish, target trophy-sized fish, or find a balance between the two, the rankings can help identify lakes that best match your fishing preference. To provide an up-to-date picture of each fishery, rankings are based on the most recent year of data available rather than calculating an average across sampling years. Although fishing success can vary with weather, season, and other environmental conditions, these rankings offer a consistent, science-based comparison of Alabama’s fisheries using sampling events from long-term monitoring data collected by ADCNR biologists.

Fish Size Groups Stock size fish are 5 inches for crappie, 7 inches for spotted bass/Alabama bass, and 8 inches for largemouth bass. Preferred size fish are 10 inches for crappie, 14 inches for spotted bass/Alabama bass, and 15 inches for largemouth bass. Memorable size fish are 12 inches for crappie, 17 inches for spotted bass/Alabama bass, and 20 inches for largemouth bass.

Catch-per-unit-effort (CPUE) measures the number of fish caught for a given amount of sampling effort, such as fish collected per hour of electrofishing. CPUE helps biologists track changes in relative abundance over time and determine whether fish populations are increasing, decreasing, or remaining stable.

Age at Length (TTR) is the time-to-reach preferred size for each species.
